Qlyno — Patient Journey
End-to-End Patient & Hospital Care Journey
Source: Qlyno Miro Patient Journey board
1. Journey Overview
This document converts the supplied Qlyno patient-journey flowchart into a structured, editable product document. It covers routine doctor discovery and appointment booking, online consultation, emergency handling, hospital admission and surgery, diagnostics, pharmacy, discharge, and automated follow-up.

3. Detailed Patient Journey
1. Patient enters the care journey
The patient can enter through an offline/in-person route, an online consultation route, or an emergency route.
2. Search for a doctor
For a normal condition, the patient looks for doctors or appointments. The search can be based on problem, speciality, doctor name, hospital, or similar criteria.
3. Choose hospital or clinic
The system can show doctors from a hospital or doctors from a clinic. The patient can then book an appointment for the selected hospital or clinic.
4. Offline appointment and reception
If the patient is coming from an offline journey, the patient reaches the receptionist. The receptionist checks the available slot and whether the patient is already registered.
5. Patient registration
If the patient is not registered, the patient is registered before proceeding. For a registered patient, the flow can continue toward the doctor OPD after slot checking.
Decision / Branch: Registered patient → continue to OPD. New patient → register first.
6. Online consultation
The patient can look for a doctor and book/enter a video or audio consultation. The patient may call a specific doctor or be connected to a random doctor who can pick up.
7. Consultation is recorded
The consultation is logged in the patient and doctor dashboard so that the clinical interaction is retained as part of the patient record.
8. Prescription or laboratory recommendation
After consultation, the doctor can suggest a prescription and/or suggest a laboratory test.
9. Medication and lab fulfillment
A prescription can lead to an order from a pharmacy. A lab recommendation can lead to a home sample collection. The patient receives the resulting medication and/or lab report.
10. Follow-up planning
The care flow includes a free follow-up period or a doctor-decided follow-up. A follow-up date can be selected and the patient can be reminded before the due date.
11. Emergency entry
For an emergency, the patient clicks the emergency option. The flow then determines whether the patient is currently being treated at a hospital or is receiving treatment from home.
12. Emergency coordination
The emergency flow sends an SOS alert to the relevant hospital and can notify the nearest ambulance and family. If the patient is already being treated at a hospital, the hospital can be alerted directly. If not, the flow can help direct the patient toward an appropriate hospital/emergency destination.
13. Hospital admission
When a patient comes to the hospital for admission, the flow moves through reception and doctor assessment. The doctor assesses the patient and guides the next clinical steps.
14. Government scheme / Ayushman eligibility
The doctor assistant or nurse can check whether the patient is eligible for a government scheme such as Ayushman. If the patient wants to avail the benefit, eligibility is checked; if eligible, the process proceeds toward the applicable ward/bed and treatment workflow.
15. Bed / ward and nursing coordination
After admission/bed allocation, the nurse receives the patient and records the relevant care information. Nursing coordination continues through treatment and preparation for surgery when required.
16. Surgery decision
If surgery is not required, the patient continues through the applicable treatment pathway. If surgery is required, the surgeon-related workflow begins.
17. Surgeon workflow
The hospital-side flow includes doctor review, a request for surgeon availability, creation/review of the surgical plan or proposal, and getting the surgeon and patient together before the operation.
18. Operation and treatment
The patient proceeds through the operation and associated treatment. Nursing staff manage the required care during the treatment period.
19. Doctor consultation and recovery
Following the operation/treatment, the doctor consultation and recovery stage determine whether the patient is ready to leave the hospital or requires continued care.
20. Discharge / patient goes home
When the patient is ready, the patient leaves the hospital and receives instructions for medication, reports, and follow-up.
21. Medication and lab reports
The patient receives the medication and relevant report. If further investigation is required, the patient can be directed to the lab and the resulting report feeds back into the care cycle.
22. Follow-up reminder
The system can send a reminder for the follow-up date. If the patient attends, the follow-up consultation occurs. If the patient does not attend, the flow can trigger another reminder or follow-up action.

8. Important Product Design Considerations
Consideration
Why it matters
Emergency reliability
Emergency workflows are high-stakes. Alerts, escalation, delivery status, fallback channels and clear responsibility should be explicitly designed and tested.
Clinical vs administrative data
The product should clearly separate clinical decisions from administrative automation. The system should support clinicians rather than silently make clinical decisions.
Patient identity
Offline registration and online profiles need a robust identity/duplicate-resolution mechanism so records do not fragment.
Consent and privacy
Video consultations, medical records, prescriptions, reports and family/emergency notifications require explicit privacy and consent controls.
Hospital interoperability
Hospital-side workflows may need integration with existing HIS/EMR systems rather than assuming every hospital will replace its current workflow.
Notifications
Follow-up and emergency notifications need delivery tracking and fallback logic; a notification that is merely 'sent' is not the same as one that was received.
9. End-to-End Summary
The Qlyno journey is designed as a connected care ecosystem rather than a single appointment booking flow. A patient can discover a doctor, book offline or online care, receive consultation, prescriptions and diagnostic services, enter an emergency pathway, move through hospital admission and surgery when required, and then continue into discharge and follow-up. The central product opportunity is to keep patient information and coordination connected across these transitions.
